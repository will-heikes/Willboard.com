About Willboard

Willboard is a weekly music chart that has run since January 2010. The Hot 20 tracks country, and the Pop 10 tracks pop. A new chart comes out every Thursday.

Each year the Willboard Awards celebrate the biggest songs, albums and artists on the charts.

How the charts work

Every chart is ordered by plays, counted week by week.

  • Debuts. A new song enters at the highest spot where its plays before charting are more than the plays of the song already there.
  • Moving down. A song falls when its plays drop below songs that charted under it the week before.
  • Ties. When two songs have the same plays, a song that fell the week before ranks below one that didn't.
  • Going recurrent. A song leaves the chart when it fell the week before and its plays are now lower than every other song on the chart. A song with the fewest plays of the week can also be moved to recurrent.

The Hot 20 has followed these rules from its first week. The Pop 10 has used the same rules since play tracking began in December 2017. Before that, its charts were put together by feel, apart from a stretch in 2014 and 2015 when Spotify's Top Songs for Me set the order.

Where the plays come from

Hot 20 plays come from Will's own iTunes library. When a song is in the library more than once, such as a single and an album copy, the copy played most carries the count. A song's play count only ever goes up. Pop 10 plays are tracked week by week, on Last.fm from December 2017 and on stats.fm since 2023.

Chart points

Every week on the chart earns a song points, based on its position. The top five spots are worth the most.

Year-End and Decade-End

Year-End charts rank the biggest songs of each year. Most years use a weighted system: a song's plays for the year as a share of the top song's plays, added to its chart points for the year as a share of the top song's points.

  • Hot 20. Through 2011, year-end ranks came from plays within the year alone. The weighted system starts in 2012.
  • Pop 10. Through 2017, year-end ranks came from chart points alone, since pop plays weren't tracked yet. The weighted system starts in 2018.
  • Decade-End. The 2010s lists use the weighted system.

Top Artists and Albums of the year

Each year's top artists and albums add up the year-end scores of their songs, using the same weighted system as the Year-End songs. An artist gets full credit for songs they're featured on, except before 2015, when a featured act got half of a song's chart points (its plays still counted in full).

Albums

  • One album, every edition. Deluxe editions, EPs, reissues and Taylor's Versions count with the original album, which gives the album its name and cover. A song first released on a Taylor's Version, such as a vault track, names that edition.
  • Every track counts. An album page lists its charted songs and every other track, and the plays of all of them add up toward the album's certification.
  • Singles and compilations aren't counted as albums.

The Willboard Awards

There are two shows each year. The Country Awards have been held since 2010, and the Pop Awards since 2013.

  • Nominees are picked from chart points and plays.
  • Country categories: Entertainer, Male Vocalist, Female Vocalist, Duo/Group, New Artist, Album, Song, Single, Musical Event and Music Video. Each has five nominees, except Duo/Group, which has had three since 2020, and Musical Event and Music Video, which have three to five.
  • Pop categories: Artist, Record, Song, Album and New Artist, plus Foreign Language Song since 2026. Each had five nominees through 2019 and has eight since 2020.
  • Pop eligibility follows the Grammys: each show honors songs from roughly the previous November through the following November, so the 2026 show covers the music of 2025.
  • New Artist means new to music. An act can be nominated more than once, but can't win if it has already won any award at an earlier show.
  • Albums can be nominated in two different years, but not again after they win.
  • Songs are only nominated in one year.

The transitional era

Willboard started with one chart. From the week of January 28, 2010 through October 20, 2011, country and pop songs charted together on the Hot 20. That stretch of 91 weeks is the transitional era. The Pop 10 launched the week of October 27, 2011, and from then on each genre had its own chart.

Records from the transitional era count on the Hot 20. On Ask Willboard you can leave them out.

Timeline

  1. 2007 to Jan 2010The Pre-CS eraCharts were made now and then, with no set method. The era ends with the first chart of the CS era. These charts come before the ones on this site.
  2. Jan 28, 2010The CS era beginsThe first weekly Hot 20, ordered by plays. CS stands for common sense: a chart every week, with the same rules every week. The transitional era begins.
  3. Oct 27, 2011The Pop 10 launchesPop gets its own chart, built in YouTube Music. The transitional era ends.
  4. Dec 2013Pop 10 moves to SpotifyCharts are built in Spotify playlists.
  5. Late 2014Top Songs for MeSpotify's Top Songs for Me sets the Pop 10 each week, until early 2015.
  6. Dec 2017Last.fm tracks Pop playsPop plays are counted every week, and the Pop 10 follows the same rules as the Hot 20.
  7. 2023stats.fm replaces Last.fmWeekly play tracking for the Pop 10 moves to stats.fm.

Charts have come out every Thursday since 2010, with two exceptions: there was no chart the weeks of August 22, 2024 and September 24, 2026.
